Aerobatics Principles · Marco “Blackbird” Merli · entry parameters and sequences only — the full explanation is in the book
FLIGHT SIMULATION USE ONLY. Fly each step until it is repeatable and relaxed before moving on. Declare your hard deck before every session and never descend below it for any reason: if a figure is not finished by the time you reach it, abandon the figure and recover.

Lines

  • Height — 4,000 ft AGL minimum. The vertical down line eats altitude fast.
  • Speed — within the green arc where possible. Vne is an absolute limit, never approached. Manage the throttle, especially on the 90° down line.
  • Load Factor — well inside the positive and negative limits.
  • Controls — Ailerons and Rudder only to level the wings between sets, or for safety.

Rolls

  • Attitude — nose well above the horizon before each sequence; throttle set to support the climb. The pitch you set is what pays for the drop that follows.
  • Controls — Ailerons alone. No Elevator, no Rudder, except for safety.
  • Execution — stop firmly at the target bank and hold it for a moment before the next angle.

Turns

  • Height — safe altitude. Speed — close to Va, in any case below Vne.
  • Bank — 60° to 80°, aircraft dependent. Load factor is 1/cos(bank): 60° = 2 G, and beyond 70° the curve rises steeply.
  • Controls — full Aileron to start and to stop the roll; pitch aft immediately to hold the vertical component of lift; small Aileron corrections to keep the plane of motion level.
  • Reference — horizon for pitch, a ground line for degrees of turn completed.

Stall

  • Height — 4,000 ft AGL minimum. The purpose is to live on the edge of the stall without ever crossing it.
  • Speed — just above Vs, where light buffeting starts. Trim for level flight, throttle to match.
  • Both cases — fly it power-on and at idle. Power-on stalls lower and more abruptly, and torque drops a wing.
  • Turning — keep the turn coordinated. Never use Rudder to tighten it.
